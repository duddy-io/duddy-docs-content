Duddy screens are built from containers nested inside each other, the same way native Android and iOS layouts are. Get the containers and their sizes right once, and the screen holds together on a small phone and a large one without further work.

## Choose a container: Row, Column or Stack

| Container | Places its children | Use it for |
|---|---|---|
| [Column](/components/layout#column) | Top to bottom. | Forms, lists of cards, most screens. |
| [Row](/components/layout#row) | Side by side, left to right. | Toolbars, an icon beside a label, a price beside a title. |
| [Stack](/components/layout#stack) | On top of each other, each at its own position. | Badges, overlays, text over an image. |
| [ScrollableColumn](/components/layout#scrollablecolumn) and [ScrollableRow](/components/layout#scrollablerow) | Like Column and Row, inside an area that scrolls. | Anything taller or wider than the space it has. |
| [CardView](/components/layout#cardview) | Top to bottom, inside a raised card with rounded corners, a border and a shadow. | A ready-made card. |

The screen itself is a vertical container, so anything you drop straight onto it stacks downwards. A screen does not scroll by itself: content taller than the phone is cut off. Put it in a ScrollableColumn, as in [Make a screen scroll](#make-a-screen-scroll).

Containers can be tapped. Row, Column, Stack and CardView have click events, and Row, Column and Stack have a **Ripple Color** for the touch feedback, so a whole row of a list can respond to a tap.

## Fill vs Auto vs a fixed size

**Width** and **Height**, in the **Dimension** group, each take one of four answers:

| Setting | What it does |
|---|---|
| **px** | Exactly this size. |
| **%** | A share of the parent's inside, after the parent's padding. |
| **Auto** | As big as the content needs, and no bigger. |
| **Fill** | Takes the space the parent has left. |

A px in the properties panel is a density-independent pixel: 1 dp on Android and 1 pt on iOS, so a size looks the same on screens with different pixel densities.

**Fill** does most of the work. In a Row, children set to Fill share the width left over by the others; in a Column, they share the height. That is how a header gives its title all the room the icons do not need. Across the other direction, Fill takes the parent's full size, less the component's own margin: a child with **Height** set to Fill in a Row is as tall as the Row.

A Column, Row or Stack starts with **Width** set to **Fill** and **Height** set to **Auto**. That is why a new Row stretches across the screen but is only as tall as its contents.

<Callout type="warning" title="Fill wraps inside a scroller">
  A scrolling container has no fixed length to share along the way it scrolls. A child set to Fill inside a ScrollableColumn is therefore as tall as its content, not as tall as the screen. Set a px height when you need one.
</Callout>

## Padding vs margin

- **Padding** is inside a component: the space between its edge and its children.
- **Margin** is outside it: the space between it and its neighbours. A margin can be negative, which lets a component reach past its parent's edge.

Rows, Columns and Stacks have no gap setting. Space their children with each child's **Margin**, and inset everything from the container's edge with the container's **Padding**. A [RecyclerView](/components/content#recyclerview) list has its own **Gap** between rows.

Choose one spacing step and keep to it, such as 4, 8, 12, 16 and 24 px. A screen reads as designed when the spacing repeats, and as improvised when it does not.

## Align and centre components

Alignment lives in the **Layout** group, as two controls:

- **Arrange Children**, on a container, packs its children at the start, centre or end of the direction it lays them out in. In a Column that is **Top**, **Middle** or **Bottom**; in a Row, **Left**, **Center** or **Right**.
- **Position in Column**, **Position in Row** and so on, on each child, place that child across the other direction. In a Column a child chooses **Left**, **Center** or **Right**; in a Row, **Top**, **Middle** or **Bottom**.

The panel shows only the direction that can change something, so you never see the same choice twice. There is no "space between" setting: to push items apart, put an empty container with **Fill** between them, as in the recipes below.

In a Stack, each child has a **Position in Stack** of its own: one of nine positions, from top left to bottom right. A child set to **Fill** in both directions covers the whole Stack, which is how a background picture sits behind other content.

## Make a screen scroll

<Steps>
  <Step>Drop a ScrollableColumn straight onto the screen.</Step>
  <Step>Set its **Height** to **Fill**, so it takes the screen's height.</Step>
  <Step>Move the screen's content inside it. Keep bars such as a TopBar or BottomNavigation outside it, so they stay put while the content scrolls.</Step>
</Steps>

## Show and hide parts of a screen

A component with **Visible** turned off is hidden and takes up no space, so the components around it close up. This is how a screen swaps an empty state for a list, or shows an error only when something fails.

**Transition**, in the **Behavior** group, animates the change: **None**, **Fade**, **Slide Up** or **Scale**. The animation is skipped when the phone asks for reduced motion.

To look at hidden states while you design, see [Drag and drop](/guides/designing/drag-and-drop).

## Right-to-left layouts

For Arabic, Persian, Hebrew and Urdu, set the screen's **Layout Direction** to **Right to Left**. The screen draws as a mirror image, with every dialog and sheet it shows: a Row's first child sits on the right, and a left alignment, margin, padding or text alignment sits on the right edge. Each piece of text keeps its own letters. See [Multilingual apps](/guides/logic/multilingual-apps).

## Layout recipes

    A **Row** with each child's **Position in Row** set to **Middle**. The title has **Width** **Fill**; each icon has **Width** **Auto**. The title takes everything the icons do not.
  </AccordionItem>
  <AccordionItem value="spread" title="Items spread across a row">
    A **Row** holding the items, with an empty container set to **Width** **Fill** between each pair. The spacers share the space left over, so the items spread evenly from edge to edge.
  </AccordionItem>
  <AccordionItem value="card" title="A card with an image and text below it">

  </AccordionItem>
  <AccordionItem value="badge" title="A badge on an avatar">

  </AccordionItem>
  <AccordionItem value="footer" title="Content at the top, a button pinned to the bottom">

## Long lists

A Column with fifty children builds fifty sets of components. Use a [RecyclerView](/components/content#recyclerview) instead: it builds a handful of rows and reuses them as they scroll, filling each one in from its **Bind Item** event. Its **Reached End** event fires when the last row comes into view, which is the place to load the next page of items.
