title: Layout and sizing
description: Rows, Columns and Stacks, what Fill and Auto mean, and how to build a screen that holds together on every device.
---

Duddy screens are built out of nested containers, the same way native layouts are. Get the
containers right and the screen works on a small phone and a tablet without you touching it
again.

## The four containers

| Container | Arranges children |
|---|---|
| **Column** | Top to bottom. Forms, lists of cards, most screens. |
| **Row** | Left to right. Toolbars, an icon beside a label, a price beside a title. |
| **Stack** | On top of each other, positioned by alignment. Badges, overlays, an image with text over it. |
| **ScrollableColumn** / **ScrollableRow** | The same as Column and Row, inside a scrolling area. |

A screen is itself a vertical container, so anything you drop straight onto it stacks
downwards.

## Sizing: a number, Auto, or Fill

Width and Height each take one of three answers.

| Setting | Means |
|---|---|
| A number (`px` or `%`) | Exactly this size. |
| **Auto** | As big as the content inside needs, and no bigger. |
| **Fill** | Take the space the parent has left. |

Fill is the one that does the work. In a Row, several children set to Fill share the
remaining width between them; in a Column, they share the height. That is how you build a
header where the title takes all the space and the icon takes only what it needs.

<Callout type="warning">
  **Inside a scrolling container, Fill means "as tall as the content" on the scrolling axis** —
  there is no fixed height to divide up. A child set to Fill inside a `ScrollableColumn` therefore
  wraps its content rather than filling the screen. Set an explicit height when you need one.
</Callout>

## Spacing

- **Padding** is inside the component — the gap between its edge and its children.
- **Margin** is outside it — the gap between it and its siblings.
- A container's **gap** spaces its children evenly, which is almost always better than
  putting a margin on each child.

Pick one spacing step and stick to it (4, 8, 12, 16, 24). Screens read as designed when the
spacing repeats and as improvised when it does not.

## Alignment

A container aligns its children along two axes: the direction it lays them out in, and the
one across it. In a Row, that is horizontal distribution and vertical alignment; in a
Column, the reverse. A Stack positions each child independently — top-left, centre,
bottom-right — which is what makes it the right container for badges and floating buttons.

## Recipes

<Accordion>
  <AccordionItem value="header" title="A header: title on the left, actions on the right">
    A **Row**, vertically centred. The title has Width **Fill**; each icon has Width **Auto**.
    The title takes everything the icons do not.
  </AccordionItem>
  <AccordionItem value="scroll" title="A screen that scrolls">
    Put a **ScrollableColumn** at the root with Height **Fill**, and everything else inside it.
    Keep bars (TopBar, BottomNavigation) outside the scroller so they stay put.
  </AccordionItem>
  <AccordionItem value="card" title="A card with an image and text below it">
    A **Column** with a background, corner radius and padding. The image has Width **Fill** and a
    fixed height; the text is **Auto** in both directions.
  </AccordionItem>
  <AccordionItem value="badge" title="A badge on an avatar">
    A **Stack**. The avatar is the first child; the badge is the second, aligned to the top-right,
    with a small margin to pull it over the edge.
  </AccordionItem>
  <AccordionItem value="footer" title="Content at the top, a button pinned to the bottom">
    A **Column** with Height **Fill**. Between the content and the button, an empty container with
    Height **Fill** — the spacer takes everything left over and pushes the button down.
  </AccordionItem>
</Accordion>

## Long lists

A Column with fifty children builds fifty components. Use a
[RecyclerView](/components/content) instead: it builds a handful of rows and reuses them as
they scroll, and fills each one in from its **Bind Item** event.
