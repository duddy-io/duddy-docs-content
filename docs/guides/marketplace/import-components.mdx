Beyond the built-in components, the marketplace holds components other developers have built and
published, such as charts, pickers, sensors and service connections. Once you import one, it sits in
your palette and behaves like any built-in component: drag it onto a screen, set its properties and
use its blocks in your logic.

## Before you start

- **You need edit access** to the project. People with view access do not see the import button.
- **Check your target platforms.** A component must be built for every platform your project
  targets. Choose them under **Target platforms** in [Project settings](/getting-started/project-settings).
- **Paid components** need a plan that includes premium components, and enough credits to buy them.
  See [Plans and limits](/account/plans-and-limits).

## Import a component

<Steps>
  <Step>Open the components palette and press **Import component** at the bottom.</Step>
  <Step>The **Add components** dialog opens. Search by name, tag or author, or browse by category.</Step>
  <Step>Press **Import** on a component. To import several at once, select them and press **Import selected**.</Step>
  <Step>The component is **added to your palette**, under its category.</Step>
</Steps>

The dialog groups what it finds into **Updates available**, **Not installed** and **Already added**,
so you can see at a glance what your project already has.

<Callout type="info">
  Type `@` and an author's name in the search to see only that author's components.
</Callout>

## What importing costs

Free components cost nothing. A paid component is charged once, from your credit balance, the first
time your account imports it. Importing it again in another project, or updating it, is free.

## Use an imported component

Drag it from the palette onto a screen like any other component. A component that draws nothing,
such as a sensor or a service connection, goes into the screen's hidden components instead.

On the canvas, an imported component may show as a labelled placeholder with its icon. Its real look
appears in [Live test](/build/live-test) and in your builds. Rebuild the test app after you import a
component, so the test app includes it.

Its events, functions and properties appear in the block editor once it is on a screen. See
[Logic blocks](/guides/logic/blocks).

## Update to a new version

Imported components never update on their own. When an author publishes a new version, the
component appears under **Updates available** in the **Add components** dialog with the version change.
Press **Update** on one, or **Update all**.

## Remove a component

Hover over the component in the palette and press the remove button, then confirm. You cannot remove
a component that is still on a screen: delete it from every screen first. Built-in components cannot
be removed.

## When Vibe mode imports for you

When you ask Vibe mode for something a built-in component cannot do, it can import a free component
from the marketplace, and only one your target platforms can build. If you never chose target
platforms and the component is built for fewer of them, the import narrows your targets to match,
and the reply tells you.

## Troubleshooting

| Message | What it means |
|---|---|
| **That component is no longer available in the marketplace.** | Its author or Duddy withdrew it. Choose another component. |
| **You don't have enough credits to buy this component.** | Top up or wait for your credits, then import again. |
| **Paid marketplace components aren't included in your plan. Upgrade to import premium components.** | Your plan does not include premium components. See [Plans and limits](/account/plans-and-limits). |
| **This component is not built for Web, which this app targets.** | Turn that platform off under **Target platforms** in Project settings, or choose another component. |
| **This component is still used on Home.** | Delete it from the screens named, then remove it from your palette. |

<Callout type="warning">
  A marketplace component runs inside your app like your own logic does. Test it in Live test before
  you ship, especially one that handles payments, accounts or personal data.
</Callout>
