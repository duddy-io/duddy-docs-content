title: Collaboration
description: Invite people to a project, edit the same canvas live, leave comments on components, and roll back to any point.

A Duddy project is multiplayer. Invite someone by email address and you are both editing
the same screens, with no branches to merge and no seats to provision.

## Invite someone

<Steps>
  <Step>Open **Settings → Collaboration** in the designer.</Step>
  <Step>Enter the person's email address and choose a role.</Step>
  <Step>They get an invitation. Accepting adds the project to their dashboard.</Step>
</Steps>

| Role | Can |
|---|---|
| **Read only** | View screens and preview the app. |
| **Read & write** | Edit screens, logic and assets. |
| **Admin** | Everything above, plus manage collaborators. |

Remove someone from the same page. The project owner cannot be removed.

## Editing at the same time

Everyone in a project sees the same canvas: cursors, selections and property changes appear
as they happen.

- **Selection is visible.** When somebody has a component selected, you can see it — which is
  usually enough to stay out of each other's way.
- **Changes arrive as they are made**, not on save. There is nothing to push or pull.
- **Read-only collaborators** can open everything and change nothing; controls that would
  write are simply inert for them.

<Callout type="info">
  Two people editing the same component at the same moment is the one case worth avoiding — the
  last write wins, as it does everywhere. Split by screen and it never comes up.
</Callout>

## Comments

Press `Alt` + `C`, or open comments from the header, and pin feedback to a specific
component rather than describing it in a chat thread. Because a comment is attached to the
component, it survives the screen being rearranged around it.

Mention a collaborator to notify them.

## History and versions

Press `Alt` + `V` for the history panel. It lists what changed, who changed it, and when —
including changes made by the AI, grouped by the turn and labelled with the prompt that
caused them.

<Steps>
  <Step>**Preview** an entry to see the screen as it was at that point, without committing to anything.</Step>
  <Step>**Restore** to bring a screen back to that state.</Step>
  <Step>**Revert an AI turn** from its card to undo everything that turn did, including screens it created.</Step>
</Steps>

Save a named version whenever you are about to try something risky. Experiments stay cheap
when undoing one is free.
