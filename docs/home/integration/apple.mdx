iOS builds have to be signed by Apple, with your account — nobody else can do it for you.
Duddy uses an **App Store Connect API key** to sign a build and, when you want it, upload
that build to TestFlight.

Without a key connected, iOS builds cannot be produced at all.

## What you need

- An **Apple Developer Program** membership (the paid one — a free Apple ID cannot sign
  distributable builds).
- An **App Store Connect API key** with enough access to manage certificates, profiles and
  builds.

## Create the key

<Steps>
  <Step>Sign in to [App Store Connect](https://appstoreconnect.apple.com/access/integrations/api) and open **Users and Access → Integrations → App Store Connect API**.</Step>
  <Step>Create a key with a role that can manage certificates and builds, and give it a name you will recognise.</Step>
  <Step>Download the `.p8` file. **Apple lets you download it once** — keep a copy somewhere safe.</Step>
  <Step>Note the **Issuer ID** shown above the key list, and the **Key ID** on the key's row.</Step>
</Steps>

<Callout type="danger">
  A `.p8` key can sign and upload builds for your account. Treat it like a password: do not commit
  it to a repository, and revoke it in App Store Connect if it ever leaks.
</Callout>

## Connect it to Duddy

<Steps>
  <Step>Open **Integrations** in your dashboard and choose **Apple Developer**.</Step>
  <Step>Paste the **Issuer ID** and the **Key ID**, and upload the `.p8` file.</Step>
  <Step>Save. iOS builds are now signed with this key.</Step>
</Steps>

## Before your first iOS build

Duddy runs a preflight against your Apple setup when you start an iOS build and tells you
what it found:

- **Warnings** are things Duddy can create for you — a missing signing certificate or a
  provisioning profile.
- **Failures** are things only you can fix. The two common ones are a key that Apple has
  rejected (expired or revoked), and no app record in App Store Connect for your package
  name.

<Callout type="info">
  Create the app record in App Store Connect with **exactly** the package name from
  [Project settings](/getting-started/project-settings). Apple calls it the Bundle ID, and the
  two have to match character for character.
</Callout>

## After the build

A finished `.ipa` can go to TestFlight for testers, and from there to review. iOS builds
cannot be installed from a link the way an Android `.apk` can — that is Apple's rule, not
Duddy's.

<Callout type="warning">
  If the integration row turns amber, Apple has rejected the key and iOS builds are paused until
  you connect a new one. Generate a fresh key in App Store Connect and reconnect.
</Callout>
