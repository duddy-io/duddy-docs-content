Vibe mode runs on models from several providers — Google, OpenAI, Anthropic, DeepSeek,
xAI, Meta, Qwen, and others — and you choose which one a turn uses. Open the settings
icon in the chat header and pick from the **Models** section.

## Chat and output are two choices

| Setting | Used for |
|---|---|
| **Chat** | Conversation, planning and reasoning — working out what to build. |
| **Output** | Writing the UI layouts and the logic blocks themselves. |

They can be the same model, and **Match chat** sets the output model back to whatever the
chat model is. Splitting them is useful when you want a strong model to plan and a fast one
to type: the plan is where most of the quality lives, and the translation step is mostly
mechanical.

## Tiers

Every model carries a tier, and your plan decides which tiers you can select.

| Tier | Who has it |
|---|---|
| **Free** | Every paid plan, whichever features it holds. |
| **Standard** | Paid plans. |
| **Premium** | The top plans — the largest, most capable models. |

The **trial is the exception**: it runs on one model — DeepSeek V4 Flash, the model a new
account already starts on — and every other model in the picker is locked, free tier
included, until you move to a paid plan. It is the same model Duddy's own pipeline uses by
default, so a trial can plan and build a whole app on it. What it cannot do is see: it is a
text-only model, so attachments and the **Ultra** detail level need a paid plan and a vision
model.

A locked model shows a badge in the picker. Selecting one you are not entitled to is
refused when the turn runs, not silently downgraded, so you always know which model
answered.

<Callout type="info">
  New accounts start on a fast, low-cost model, and on the trial it is the only one — a paid
  plan is what opens the rest of the picker for a whole app or a hard layout.
</Callout>

## What the badges mean

- **Relative cost (`×`)** — how expensive a model is against the picker's baseline model.
  A `4×` model costs roughly four times as much per turn as a `1×` one for the same work.
- **Vision** — the model can look at images. Required for attaching screenshots and sketches,
  and for the **Ultra** detail level, which reviews a render of the screen it just built.
- **Thinking** — the model can reason before answering. Some models offer graded levels
  (Low / Medium / High), others are simply on or off, and a few cannot think at all. The
  Thinking control shows only the levels the selected model actually supports.

## Picking one

<Steps>
  <Step>**Questions, small edits, renames** — a free-tier model is fine, and it is the fastest.</Step>
  <Step>**A screen you care about** — a mid-tier model with Thinking on Low or Medium.</Step>
  <Step>**A whole app, or a hard layout** — a premium model as the Chat model, Thinking Medium or High, and Detail on Normal or Ultra.</Step>
  <Step>**Working from a screenshot or a sketch** — any vision model; without one the attachment button stays off.</Step>
</Steps>

<Callout type="warning">
  Thinking and Detail multiply what a turn costs. If credits matter more than the last 10% of
  polish, turn Thinking down before you switch to a cheaper model — deliberation is usually the
  larger share of the bill.
</Callout>

## The catalogue changes

Models are added, re-priced and retired by their providers, so the picker in the builder is
the authoritative list — it shows what you can select today, with each model's tier, cost
multiplier and capabilities. This page describes how the choice works, not which models
exist this week.
