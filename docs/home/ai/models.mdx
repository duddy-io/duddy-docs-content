Vibe mode runs on models from several providers — Google, OpenAI, Anthropic, DeepSeek,
xAI, Meta, Qwen, and others — and you choose which ones a turn uses. Open the settings
icon in the chat header and pick from the **Models** section.

## Design and build are two picks

| Setting | Used for |
|---|---|
| **Design model** | The design direction and the look of every screen — what you will actually see. |
| **Build model** | Planning, turning the design into components and logic, wiring the blocks, and the review. |

They can be the same model, but they are not interchangeable: the design model decides how
a screen looks, and the build model copies that decision out. **Spend on the design model.**
On the same request, a premium build model produced identical output to a cheap one for
sixty times the price — the translation step is mechanical, and the cheapest capable model
is usually the right answer there.

## Tiers

Every model carries a tier, and your plan decides which tiers you can select.

| Tier | Who has it |
|---|---|
| **Free** | Every plan, including the trial — one model: DeepSeek V4 Flash. |
| **Standard** | Paid plans. |
| **Premium** | The top plans — the largest, most capable models. |

The free tier is deliberately one model, and it is the one Duddy's own pipeline runs by
default — so a plan with no model features still plans and builds whole apps, on exactly
the model the builder would have chosen for you. It is text-only, and that does not hold
you back: attach a screenshot and Duddy swaps a vision-capable model in for that turn, on
any plan, and says so on the turn's status line.

A locked model shows a badge in the picker. Selecting one you are not entitled to is
refused when the turn runs, not silently downgraded, so you always know which model
answered.

<Callout type="info">
  New accounts start on the free-tier model and it is the only one they can select — a paid
  plan is what opens the rest of the picker.
</Callout>

## What the badges mean

- **Relative cost (`×`)** — how expensive a model is against the picker's baseline model.
  A `4×` model costs roughly four times as much per turn as a `1×` one for the same work.
- **Vision** — the model can look at images. Not a requirement: a model that cannot see is
  swapped for one that can whenever a turn carries an image or runs the Best-quality review.
- **Thinking** — the model can reason before answering. Some models offer graded levels
  (Low / Medium / High), others are simply on or off, and a few cannot think at all. The
  Thinking control shows only the levels the selected model actually supports.

## Picking one

<Steps>
  <Step>**Questions, small edits, renames** — the free-tier model is fine, and it is the fastest.</Step>
  <Step>**A screen you care about** — a standard design model with Quality on Balanced.</Step>
  <Step>**A whole app, or a hard layout** — a premium model as the design model, Quality on Best with the designer open, Thinking Medium or High; keep the build model cheap.</Step>
  <Step>**Working from a screenshot or a sketch** — whatever you have picked. A vision model is substituted for the turn if yours cannot see.</Step>
</Steps>

<Callout type="warning">
  Quality and Thinking multiply what a turn costs. If credits matter more than the last 10% of
  polish, turn Thinking down before you switch to a cheaper model — deliberation is usually the
  larger share of the bill.
</Callout>

## The catalogue changes

Models are added, re-priced and retired by their providers, so the picker in the builder is
the authoritative list — it shows what you can select today, with each model's tier, cost
multiplier and capabilities. This page describes how the choice works, not which models
exist this week.
