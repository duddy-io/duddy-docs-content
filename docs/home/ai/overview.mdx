title: Vibe mode
description: Describe a screen, an edit or a whole app in the chat, and Duddy builds it into your project as ordinary components and blocks.

Vibe mode is the AI chat in the designer. You describe what you want, it builds it — a
section on the screen you are looking at, a set of new screens, the logic behind a button,
or a new look for the whole app.

What it writes is not a special AI layer. It is the same component tree and the same
blocks you would have made by hand, so every one of them can be selected, dragged,
renamed and rewired afterwards.

## What a turn can do

Duddy reads your message, decides what kind of turn you asked for, and runs the matching
pipeline.

| Kind | Example |
|---|---|
| **Answer** | "What does a Stack do?" — a question is answered and nothing is changed. |
| **Edit** | "Make the card corners rounder and the price bold." — one screen, its UI or its logic. |
| **Section** | "Add a filter bar under the title." — new UI on the current screen. |
| **Logic** | "When Sign in is tapped, validate the email and go to Home." |
| **Restyle** | "Make the whole app dark and teal." — a new look, and every screen redrawn to it. |
| **New screens** | "Add a checkout screen and an order confirmation screen." |
| **Whole app** | "A recipe app with a browse screen, search, and a saved list." |

A request that has nothing to do with the app — an email, a song — is declined in one short
step. On a bigger request the AI may ask you one question before it starts; answer it, or
press **Just pick for me**.

If you would rather choose the route yourself, type `/` in the chat and pick a command:

| Command | Does |
|---|---|
| `/edit` | Change something already on screen. |
| `/section` | Design new UI on the current screen. |
| `/screens` | Design one or more new screens. |
| `/logic` | Wire behaviour on components that already exist. |
| `/figma` | Attach one of your [Figma](/integration/figma) designs to build screens from. |
| `/imagine` | Generate an image straight into your assets. |

There is no command for the theme, an asset or a screen rename: each is one edit in the
designer's own panels, and a sentence about the look of the app is read as a restyle like
any other message.

## Watching it build

A build turn settles the look first — palette, type, spacing, the parts every screen
shares — then plans the screens, then builds each one: its tree, then its behaviour a few
handlers at a time. Screens appear on the canvas as they finish — you do not wait for the
whole app before you can look at anything. The photos a screen needs are found on Pixabay
and its illustrations drawn by the image model, so screens arrive with their pictures.

You can keep working while it runs. If you close the tab, the turn carries on; only the
review described under Settings needs the designer open.

## Settings

Open the settings icon in the chat header. The choices are genuinely different knobs
rather than one quality slider:

| Setting | What it changes |
|---|---|
| **Design model** | Decides what your screens look like — it runs the design direction. This is the pick worth upgrading. See [Models](/ai/models). |
| **Build model** | Turns that design into components and logic. It transcribes; it decides nothing about the look, so the cheapest capable model is usually right. |
| **Quality** | How much the AI thinks before each step. **Fast** halves the usual thinking, **Balanced** is the default, **Best** doubles it and adds a review of every screen. Every answer is checked the same way at every level. |
| **UI style** | The look it defaults to. See [UI style](/ai/ui-style). |
| **Thinking** | Under Advanced: multiplies the Quality budget — Off, or Low/Medium/High where the model grades it, On/Off where it does not. |

<Callout type="info" title="Best quality reviews what it drew">
  On **Best**, after each screen lands your designer draws it and a vision model checks the
  render for overlap, clipping, poor contrast and misalignment, fixing what it finds as one
  more change of the turn. The review runs only while your designer tab is open for the
  project — close it and the turn finishes without the review.
</Callout>

Bigger settings cost more credits and take longer. A wording tweak does not need Best and
High; a first-draft app screen usually deserves them.

## Giving it context

- **Attach an image** — a screenshot, a photo of a whiteboard, a competitor's screen. Paste
  it into the chat or use the attachment button. Under 1 MB, on any model: if the model you
  picked cannot see images, Duddy swaps one in that can for that turn and says so on the
  turn's status line.
- **Draw it** — sketch the layout you want in the built-in drawing pad. See
  [Attach drawings](/ai/attach-drawings).
- **Point at a design** — `/figma` attaches one of your Figma files, a section of it or a
  single frame, and it stays attached to every message until you remove it.

## Sessions

Chats are saved per project. Start a new chat when you move to a different piece of work —
the old conversation stays in the history where you can reopen it, so nothing is thrown
away to get a clean slate.

## When it gets something wrong

Everything an AI turn does arrives as ordinary project history, so you have three ways out:

<Steps>
  <Step>Tell it. "Not that — keep the layout, just change the palette" is usually one cheap turn.</Step>
  <Step>Fix it by hand on the canvas. Nothing it wrote is locked.</Step>
  <Step>Undo the turn. Every turn's card in the chat lists what it changed, and **Undo it** puts the whole turn back, including screens it created. The history panel (`Alt` + `V`) shows the same turns.</Step>
</Steps>

## What it costs

Every turn spends credits, charged by what the turn actually used — see
[Credits and plans](/getting-started/credits-and-plans). If a turn is refused, the chat
says which of the six reasons it was: the model you picked is not in your plan, your balance
is empty, today's limit is used up, requests still running hold the credits it needs,
billing could not be reached, or Vibe is switched off for your plan — and what to do about
each.
