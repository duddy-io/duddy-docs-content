A release build gives you a signed app file. What happens next depends on where the app is going:
a tester's phone, an iPhone registered to your Apple account, or a store. This page sets out what
Duddy does for you today, what you do yourself, and what is coming soon.

## Where your app can go today

| Destination | What Duddy gives you | Today |
|---|---|---|
| Android phones, yours or your testers' | A signed `.apk` to scan, download or link | Works |
| iPhones registered to your Apple Developer account | An ad hoc `.ipa` that installs over the air | Beta |
| Your phone while you design | A test app that follows every edit, with [Live test](/build/live-test) | Works |
| Google Play | Google Play needs an Android App Bundle (`.aab`) for a new app | Coming soon |
| TestFlight and the App Store | Duddy builds ad hoc only and uploads nothing to App Store Connect | Not available |
| The web | Web builds and hosting | Coming soon |

## Send an Android build to testers

<Steps>
  <Step>Build **Android App (.apk)** and wait for the console to say **Ready**. See [Build the app](/getting-started/build-the-app).</Step>
  <Step>Press **Download app** to save the `.apk`. The download link lasts one hour, so share the file rather than the link.</Step>
  <Step>Send the file the way you share any file, such as by email or a shared drive.</Step>
  <Step>Each tester opens the `.apk` on their phone and allows installs from that app when Android asks.</Step>
</Steps>

To send an update, raise the **Version code** in **Settings → Project**, build again and send the new
file. It installs over the earlier version, keeping the tester's data, because the package name and
the signing key stay the same.

## Install an iOS build on testers' iPhones

<Badge type="beta" /> An ad hoc iOS build installs only on iPhones that were registered to your Apple Developer account
when it was built, and that run iOS 26 or later.

<Steps>
  <Step>Ask each tester for their iPhone's UDID, and register it at developer.apple.com under **Certificates, Identifiers & Profiles → Devices**.</Step>
  <Step>Build **iOS App (.ipa)**. A build made after the registration includes the new iPhones. See [iOS builds](/build/ios-builds).</Step>
  <Step>When the console says **Ready**, the tester scans the QR code under **Install this build** with the iPhone's camera and follows the prompt to install. The code works for one hour.</Step>
</Steps>

The QR code suits a tester who can see your screen. **Download app** saves the `.ipa` itself if
you prefer to install it with your own tools.

## Let someone try the app while you design

You do not need a release build to show work in progress. Pair the other person's phone with
[Live test](/build/live-test): they scan the QR code in the Live test panel to install the test
app, then type the 8-character code it shows. Their phone then follows your edits as you make them.
An iPhone has to be registered to your Apple Developer account, as for any iOS build.

## Publish to Google Play

Coming soon. Google Play requires a new app to be uploaded as an Android App Bundle (`.aab`).
Duddy builds `.apk` files today; the **Android App Bundle (.aab)** option in the Build menu says
"This part of the builder is still being built." Until it arrives, Duddy cannot produce a file that
Google Play accepts for a new app.

You can prepare now, so the first upload goes smoothly when it does:

- **Settle the package name** before anyone installs the app. See
  [Keep your app's identity stable](#keep-your-apps-identity-stable).
- **Keep the signing key** your first Android build creates. Every update must be signed with it.
- **Plan your version numbers**: the **Version code** must rise with every upload.
- **Build on a paid plan** for the release, so the app carries no Duddy branding.

The Google Play developer account, the store listing and Google's review happen in Google Play
Console, outside Duddy.

## Publish to the App Store

Not available. Duddy's iOS builds are ad hoc builds for registered devices. TestFlight and the App
Store need a build signed for App Store distribution and uploaded to App Store Connect. Duddy does
not make that kind of build and does not upload anything to App Store Connect, so you cannot
publish to TestFlight or the App Store from Duddy today.

What you set up for ad hoc builds still counts: the Bundle ID you registered is the same identifier
an App Store app uses.

## Keep your app's identity stable

A store recognises your app by its package name and, on Android, by the key it is signed with.
Change either and the store sees a different app. Set these in the designer before you share the
app widely:

| Setting | Where | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| **Package name** | **Settings → Project** | The app's permanent identifier, such as `com.yourcompany.yourapp`. Apple calls it the Bundle ID. A new package name makes a new app. |
| Signing key | **Settings → Keystore & profile** | Your first Android release build creates a signing key and keeps it with the project. Every later build is signed with it, so updates install over earlier versions. |
| **Version code** | **Settings → Project** | A whole number that must rise with every store upload. |
| **Version name** | **Settings → Project** | What people see on the store listing, such as `1.2.0`. |

<Callout type="danger" title="Do not delete your signing key">
  Builds signed with a different key cannot update an app already installed on phones or already
  on Google Play. Leave the key that your first Android build created in place.
</Callout>

## Remove Duddy branding before you ship

Apps built on the free trial carry Duddy branding: they open on a Duddy splash screen when they
have no splash image of their own, and Android apps show a **Made with Duddy** badge. Builds made
on a paid plan carry neither. Compare plans on [Plans and limits](/account/plans-and-limits).
